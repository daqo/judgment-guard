# Judgment Guard Rubric
Level definitions for the seven dimensions rated in Step 5 of `SKILL.md`. Consult this when a rating is borderline. Every rating must cite specific claim-map rows, not a general impression.

## Borderline calls
- Evidence strength selects the intervention tier (high → Tier A, medium → Tier B, low → Tier C), so it is the rating that matters most. When torn between two levels, count: if any load-bearing claim sits in *Speculation / missing evidence*, the rating cannot be high. If most load-bearing claims sit in *Supported facts* and none in speculation, it cannot be low.
- Recall gate: evidence strength cannot be high if any source-type matrix row is blank, if any load-bearing claim lacks a disconfirming query, or if no search tool was available. Strength measures what was found *and* how hard you looked for what would contradict it.
- Stale evidence caps evidence strength at medium unless recency has been verified.
- A rating you cannot justify from the claim map is a sign the claim map is incomplete. Go back to Step 3.

## Evidence strength
High:
- multiple specific supporting facts, each with a ledger entry (origin, date, type)
- at least two independent origins for each load-bearing claim
- disconfirming queries run and their results accounted for
- no major unresolved contradiction
- evidence is current and not outdated by changed conditions

Medium:
- partial support
- some assumptions needed
- important unknowns remain
- one or more source-type rows searched with nothing found
- no search tool was available (hard cap)

Low:
- mostly generalized reasoning
- little direct support; claims rest on prior knowledge retrieval did not confirm
- strong claims built on weak premises
- evidence may be stale or from outdated context

## Assumption visibility
High:
- key assumptions stated explicitly
- load-bearing premises identified
- reader can see what the advice depends on

Medium:
- some assumptions implied but not called out
- premises partially visible

Low:
- assumptions invisible
- conclusions presented as though premises are self-evident

## Confidence calibration
Overstated:
- strong certainty words without support
- elegant causal chain with omitted caveats
- one-sided framing
- recommendation stronger than evidence
- uncertain claims stated without qualification

Appropriate:
- certainty matches evidence quality
- hedges present where needed, absent where not
- caveats included for weak spots without burying strong conclusions
- at the claim level, qualifiers present where evidence is uncertain and absent where solid

Underconfident:
- well-supported claims buried in unnecessary qualifiers
- useful conclusions presented tentatively despite strong evidence
- excessive hedging that undermines actionability

## Action pressure
High:
- "you should"
- ranked recommendation
- urgency framing
- executive-summary style conclusion

Medium:
- preference implied but not absolute

Low:
- exploratory and comparative

## Alternative coverage
High:
- multiple plausible options presented
- trade-offs compared
- no viable option ignored

Medium:
- some alternatives mentioned but not developed

Low:
- single option presented as obvious choice
- alternatives ignored or dismissed

## Epistemic authority
Appropriate:
- reasoning presented as general analysis
- no implied domain expertise
- defers to professionals where warranted

Overstated:
- implies insider or professional knowledge
- presents general reasoning as expert opinion
- fails to flag when professional advice is needed

## False consensus
None detected:
- disagreements among sources surfaced
- tensions between data points preserved
- reader can see where sources conflict

Possible:
- disagreement acknowledged but minimized or downplayed
- conflicting evidence mentioned but not given appropriate weight

Present:
- conflicting sources merged into single narrative without surfacing tension
- disagreement between data points erased in synthesis
- false agreement manufactured from genuinely conflicting inputs
